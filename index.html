FIELD NOTES / 001
PASSIVE BISTATIC RADARCONCEPT GEOMETRY

DISSERTATION / FEASIBILITY STUDY

Listening to the sky.

Passive radar for Glasgow airspace

Could an existing over-the-horizon transmission be used to detect aircraft near Glasgow? A study of passive bistatic radar, bringing together propagation, geometry, and signal processing.

Link budgetsSignal processingMATLAB
Inside the project +

I investigated a non-cooperative illuminator, estimated received signal levels and target returns, and explored matched filtering and pulse integration.

The work was a feasibility study: assessing the assumptions and detection constraints, rather than claiming an operational aircraft-tracking system.

002 / AUTONOMY

ACADEMIC PROJECT

Autonomous collection robot

An ESP32-based robot connecting sensing, navigation, and a collection mechanism through a real-time state machine.

ESP32FreeRTOSI²C
Inside the project +

Coordinated ultrasonic sensing, steering, motor control, and a magnetic collector. An ESP32-S3 camera unit communicated over I²C, bringing vision and control tasks together.

Focus: turning sensor inputs into predictable behaviour, with explicit states for approach, identification, and collection.

003 / COMPUTATION

ACADEMIC PROJECT

FPGA fractal visualisation

Exploring hardware acceleration through fractal generation, with a Python and Jupyter interface for experimentation.

FPGAPythonJupyter
Inside the project +

Used programmable logic to explore the relationship between mathematical computation and hardware implementation.

The project connected FPGA-based processing with a software interface, developing an understanding of how work can be shared between hardware and software.

004 / SMALL-SCALE SYSTEMS

PERSONAL PROJECT · IN DEVELOPMENT

Model railway lighting

A compact controller per building, combining shared 5 V power, LED switching, and a distributed communications bus.

STM32KiCadRS-485
Inside the project +

The design uses an STM32C011, 3.3 V regulation, low-side MOSFET LED switching, and local mode selection.

Focus: compact hardware, sensible power distribution, and repeatable control across multiple model buildings. Hardware and firmware remain in development.

03 / THE ENGINEER BEHIND THE WORK

BASED IN SCOTLAND

Curious by nature.
Practical by training.

I’m a graduate electrical engineer with a BEng (Hons) in Electrical & Electronic Engineering from the University of Strathclyde.

My current work sits in electrical building services and infrastructure: cable calculations, lighting, MV and LV drawings, arc-flash work, and earthing. It’s a field that brings analysis together with real constraints — space, coordination, maintainability, and reliability.

Before that, I gained hands-on industrial controls experience during an internship in refrigeration engineering. Working with PLCs, HMIs, and plant communications helped me understand what happens between a design on paper and equipment in operation.

Outside work, I keep building. Embedded electronics, robotics, and PCB projects give me space to experiment and keep learning from the detail.
